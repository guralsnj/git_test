# Territory review: audit and slide structure

Prepared for Nate Guralski, September 24, 2026. Sources are September meeting notes and transcripts (Krisp and Pocket). Numbers are as spoken in those meetings. Verify against Don's monthly activity report and the ranked customer workbook before showing any of them.

## The short version

The lead time excuse is half true. Lead times were 30-plus weeks in the first half and dealers went elsewhere. They have been back to 5 to 10 weeks since summer, and the plants were not full before Walmart. So the question for every territory is not "did lead time hurt you" but "what happened when it came back." The bigger story is Excalibur. The program carries a third of the business historically and did 19 percent last year, with some Excaliburs at zero this quarter. The dealer scorecard slide is where that gets surfaced, territory by territory, with numbers you pre-fill.

## What the record says

- First half 2026 lead times were 30-plus weeks. Don, Sept 2: dealers can't wait thirty weeks, so all of them were going elsewhere. Lead time for the first half really hurt us.
- Lead times now. Aug 31 EC L10: six weeks, ten weeks, five to six weeks by plant. Sept 8 major projects: Stevens Point shipping mid October, New London early November, Rome late November. New London full through October, Rome at 80 percent.
- The counter. Don, Sept 2: the other sales managers were upset Walmart took up all that capacity, but they weren't filling the plants with what they had, mostly the Excaliburs. Brian, Sept 9: I don't see the lead time thing as the issue.

- Material. Tube availability on curtailment is the constraint now named at the L10, not floor capacity.

Excalibur

- About 29 to 30 Excalibur dealers, 19 of them in Kurt's territory. Four stocking dealers.
- Excaliburs did $19M last year, 19 percent of the business, against a historical third (Don, Sept 2). Brian, Aug 31: Excalibur sales down 50 percent 2025 versus 2024. Nate to Colin, Sept 22: down about 70 percent in three years.
- Don's Q3 Access report shows Excaliburs with zero bookings, quoting, or revenue (Nate, Sept 9 tactical L10).
- Excalibur forecasts, which set the budgets, have missed two years running (Brian, Aug 31).
- No written qualification criteria. Brian: I don't know what the requirements are to be an effective Excalibur. Requirements were lowered for some and raised for others. Container Systems and Cox are on the list without the revenue to be there.
- Dealer council quote, relayed by Brian: we don't understand or see the value of an Excalibur.
- Protection rule: if an Excalibur has quoted, nobody else gets quoted, even another Excalibur. Brian, Sept 9: that stops. It has blocked Apex, which does $40M with Nucor and $5M with us.
- Brian's direction: three tiers, a revenue component, stocking points, and a program that means something when we sit down with them.

## Slide structure, final (revised email, Sept 24)

Superseded the seven-slide draft. Six slides, ten minutes, pricing left for Day 2. Three decks: RSM, Chad (Walmart, Sam's, Ambient), Mac (national accounts). Excalibur appears only in the RSM deck.

1. In numbers. Bookings and quotes by quarter, top accounts, Excalibur dealers (RSM only). Pre-filled. Two sentences on what the numbers don't show.
2. Who's winning with what. RSM: top dealers, Excalibur flag, products they perform with, why they pick Steel King. Chad: products performing across Walmart and why Walmart keeps choosing us. Mac: products winning by account, and the biggest new customer opportunities.
3. What they buy us for, and what's missing. Two or three things they count on us for first, then the one gap that costs the most. Mac's version is why a national account would pick us.
4. Mindshare. RSM: how you get and keep it, how new dealer reps get up to speed, what's working. Chad: inside Walmart and with their integrators. Mac: how you're getting in the door.
5. Three wins, one loss. Job, competitor, what won it, repeatable. For the loss: what decided it and what else was true, what we could have done differently. The word price is not on the slide.
6. Q4. Three projects, one dealer or account to grow, one thing you'd change about how we run sales.

Presenter order: Jim or whoever has the cleanest win story goes first. Kurt doesn't go first.
