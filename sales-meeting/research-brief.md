# Research brief: lead follow-up, first touch, and customer service in industrial B2B

Prepared for the Steel King sales meeting, September 2026. Companion to `Speed_and_Service_Win_the_Order.pptx`.

Every number in the deck is in this brief with its source, year, URL, and a confidence grade. Section 8 lists popular statistics that were checked and left out because they cannot be traced to a real study.

**Confidence key**

- **Primary**: read directly from the publisher's own report, PDF, or press release.
- **Primary (indirect)**: the publisher's site blocked fetching; figure confirmed from the publisher's own wire release or two or more independent outlets quoting it verbatim.
- **Secondary**: reproduced only by a third party.
- **Unverified**: cannot be traced to an original. Not used in the deck.
- **Dated**: more than ten years old. Used only where no newer B2B-specific figure exists, and flagged.

## 8. Popular statistics checked and left out

These circulate on LinkedIn and in sales training decks. None traces to a study that can be produced. They are not in the deck.

| Popular claim | What the trail shows |
|---|---|
| "80% of sales require 5 follow-ups; 44% of reps give up after one." | The Brevet Group (2014) cites Marketing Donut, which cited nothing. Sales & Marketing Executives International traced the 80%/5-calls figure in 2021 to a 1942 survey of fewer than 40 members of a Long Island sales club selling door to door and by phone. The 44/22/14/12% give-up breakdown has no traceable origin at all. Also recycled as "80% of deals close after the 5th touchpoint (Salesforce)." |
| "78% of customers buy from the first company that responds." | Attributed to a "Lead Connect survey" with no published report or methodology anywhere. Also mis-credited to Drift, Vendasta, and HBR. |
| "35 to 50% of sales go to the vendor that responds first." | An InsideSales blog assertion ("sales studies consistently show") with no citation. Sometimes attributed to a Google/CEB white paper that does not contain it. |
| "The MIT study: 21x more likely to qualify at 5 minutes vs 30." | Real data, wrong label. It is the 2007 InsideSales.com study by Dr. James Oldroyd, then an MIT Sloan fellow. Vendor-sponsored, never published by MIT. HBR 2011 (7x and 60x) is the clean citation. |
| "391% higher conversion when contacted within 1 minute." | Real (Velocify 2012) but from mortgage, insurance, and education phone sales, not B2B. Frequently misattributed to Vendasta, LeanData, or HBR. |
| "Average lead response time is 47 hours." | HBR 2011 says 42 hours. The 47 appears to be a transcription error that has been copied for a decade. |
| "A dissatisfied customer tells 9 to 15 people; 13% tell 20 or more." | Attributed to the "White House Office of Consumer Affairs," which never published it. The underlying research is TARP, 1979 and 1986. No original document with that wording exists online. |

| "A 5% increase in retention increases profit 25 to 95% (Bain)." | Composite of two Reichheld papers from 1990 and 2001. The 2001 Bain figure is "more than 25%" in financial services only. |
| "80% of B2B buyers would switch suppliers if a performance guarantee was not met (McKinsey)." | Not found. McKinsey's verified figures are that ~80% say a performance guarantee is critical for loyalty (2022) and 54% would switch after a poor omnichannel experience (2024). |

## 9. Gaps in the research

- No public survey quantifies the share of U.S. pallet rack sales that flows through dealers vs direct. The 86% lift truck figure is the closest hard number for the material handling channel.
- No published percentage of industrial buyers who abandon a supplier over slow RFQ response. Thomasnet gives the 24-hour expectation and the "follow up twice" quote; Aleran gives lost-deal rates from the seller side (vendor-sponsored).
- Thomasnet's State of North American Manufacturing series appears to have stopped after 2021 (Thomas was acquired by Xometry).
- MHEDA's distributor statistical comparison and its new quarterly sentiment survey are member-only; no public figures on quote turnaround or lead-time expectations.
- Most lead-response audits (Drift, Chili Piper, RevenueHero, Workato) test B2B software companies. HBR 2011 is the only one with a mixed B2C/B2B industrial sample. The pattern is consistent across all of them.
- Most B2B buyer-behavior research (6sense, TrustRadius, Gartner regret) skews toward technology purchases. McKinsey's B2B Pulse (cross-industry including industrial) and UPS Industrial Buying Dynamics are the closest to capital equipment buying.
